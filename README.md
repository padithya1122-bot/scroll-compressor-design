Scroll Compressor Design And Iterations

This repository documents the mechanical design, CAD modeling, iterative prototyping, and development of a scroll compressor component.

Project Overview

The project covers the complete design evolution of a scroll compressor, transitioning from initial testing hurdles such as shaft misalignment and thermal melting to robust iterative solutions and a final semi-hermetic architecture.

Main Topics & Key Calculation Parameters

Geometry Design

Scroll Profile: Utilizes a Two-Arc Discharge scroll profile consisting of a Fixed Scroll and an Orbiting Scroll for continuous fluid compression.

Working Principle: The orbiting scroll moves inside the fixed scroll, progressively reducing pocket volume to raise refrigerant pressure.

Material Selection & Properties (Aluminium 4032 Alloy)
The scroll components are engineered using Aluminium 4032 Alloy, chosen for its favorable thermal and mechanical properties:

Young's Modulus: 73,000 N/mm²

Yield Strength: 280 N/mm²

Density: 2.69 g/cc

Poisson’s Ratio: 0.33

Design Evolution & Iterative Improvements

Initial Design: Held with an unstable clamp and an Oldham's coupling, which suffered from high-RPM instability and severe shaft misalignment.

Second Iteration: Replaced the Oldham's coupling with a more stable jaw coupling and bolted the compressor body directly to the base plank.

Third Iteration: Replaced melting plastic sleeves with high-RPM ball bearings to resolve frictional heat generation and tolerance stack-up issues that previously caused components to fuse.

Fourth Iteration: Designed a dedicated motor housing casing and incorporated a bottom alignment bar to permanently eliminate lateral and angular misalignment.

Semi-Hermetic Model: Fully enclosed the motor inside a sealed housing along with an integrated discharge valve and discharge chamber for advanced operation.
